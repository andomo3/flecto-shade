# D1: the evidence chart, a re-plot of a published soil moisture and disease result

Written on 2026-09-20 from the research note `../../docs/research/soil-telemetry-datasets/irrigation-moisture-disease-and-salinity-datasets.md`.
Owner: Ameya.
Optional, and it is handed over only after H3 and G1 are on `main`.
It needs no other package, and no other package needs it.

## Goal

Show, from real public field data, that wetter soil goes with more plant disease, as one chart the speaker can point at in the "what comes next" beat.
It backs one sentence of the pitch, that a per-bed record of water is worth keeping, and it claims nothing about the roof.

## Inputs

Three CSV files from Donald, Green, Searle, Cunniffe, and Purse, served by Zenodo, record 4086316, licence CC0 1.0, no login.

The address of each is `https://zenodo.org/records/4086316/files/[FILE]?download=1`.

Facts checked in the real files by the planner, with pandas 2.2.3:

- One row is one survey quadrat, and each was surveyed once, so this is not a time series.
- The three columns used are `Area.of.juniper`, `Area.of.symptoms`, and `Mean.soil.moisture`, spelt the same in all three files.
- None of the three columns holds an empty cell, and `Area.of.juniper` is never 0, so no row is dropped.
- `Area.of.symptoms` over `Area.of.juniper` runs from 0 to 1 in every file.
- `Mean.soil.moisture` runs from 32.88 to 107.76 at Birk Fell, 28.18 to 84.18 at Glenartney, and 15.33 to 52.51 at North Rothiemurchus.
  Its unit was not read from the paper, so no unit is ever printed.
- Other columns differ between the files, in name and in number, so read only the three columns above and never concatenate whole files.

## Outputs

`data/processed/evidence-juniper.csv`, committed, six rows, two a site:

```text
site,half,quadrats,moisture_median,share_symptomatic
Birk Fell,drier,23,63.51,0.1689
```

- `half` is `drier` or `wetter`, split at the site's own median of `Mean.soil.moisture`.
  A quadrat at the median goes to the drier half.
- `share_symptomatic` is the mean over the half's quadrats of `Area.of.symptoms` over `Area.of.juniper`, rounded to four places.

`software/page/evidence.html`, committed, generated by the same run, a page on its own that nothing else links to yet.

- One inline SVG drawn by the script, no chart library, no script tag, and no outside request of any kind.
- Three groups of two bars, one group a site, the drier half and the wetter half, one hue for each half, each bar labelled directly with its share as a whole percent, and no legend to decode.
- The heading: "Wetter soil, more disease: a published result, re-plotted".
- The caption, word for word: "Re-plot of public field data from Donald et al. 2020, Forest Ecology and Management 473, 118324, licence CC0. Wetter quadrats show a higher share of symptomatic juniper in all three sites. Association only. Not our data, not a shade house, and not a claim about the roof."
- Under it, one line: "Wild juniper in Britain, one survey a quadrat, 147 quadrats. The link is modest: the rank correlation across all quadrats is about 0.18."
- It reuses `style.css` by a relative address, and adds no rule to it.

## Steps

1. `data/build_evidence.py` reads the three raw files and writes `data/processed/evidence-juniper.csv`. Check: the tests below.
   If a raw file is missing or its sha256 differs, it exits non zero and names the file.
2. The same run writes `software/page/evidence.html` from the CSV it has just written. Check: every percent on the page equals the CSV's share times 100, rounded, in a test that reads both.
3. A person opens `evidence.html` from the local server with the network off, and reads the six bars against the table below.

## Acceptance

- `python data/build_evidence.py` exits 0 with the network off.
- `pytest software/tests/test_evidence.py` exits 0.

## Honesty labels

- The page says whose data it is, that it is a re-plot, and that it is an association.
- Never said, on the page or aloud: that irrigation causes disease, that the result carries over to Florida, to container media, or to any crop on our page, that the team found the link, or that the roof prevents, reduces, or detects disease.
- The roof's own figures stay "simulated", and this page holds none of them.
