# Pitch script, Plan B - [project name]

The script for the simulated demo, chosen on 2026-09-19.
`script.md` is the live demo script and is kept as the record, and this file is the one that is rehearsed.

One sentence: a rider waiting in the Houston sun gets a bus stop roof of hingeless leaves that bend into shade on their own, shown as one real leaf driven by a simulation of the whole stop, built from our CAD and a real Houston day.

Booth or stage: booth, expo style, judges come to the table for a few minutes.
Speaker: abba drives. Clicks: the judge presses play and drags the cloud, abba only if they decline. Q&A by area: the engineers take the leaf and the CAD, abba takes the simulation, the data, and the numbers.

Placeholders to fill or verify before this is said out loud.
Rosa is an illustrative name and the team may change it.
[X] is the share of the sun's direct light reaching the bench in the harsh hours of the demo day with the leaves bent, and [Y] is the same with the leaves left resting, both from the simulation.
[W] is the share of the cool months' direct light that reaches the bench under the open leaves and that a fixed slab of the same footprint blocks, from package B6.
All three are simulated, and none is ever called measured.
The study figures and the one in six come from `story.md`, which lists each source.
If package B15 ships, "drag that cloud over the sun" becomes "put your hand over that sensor: it is real, and your hand is the cloud".
If the real leaf is not running, the two sentences about it are dropped and nothing else changes.

What changed from the live script, and why.
"Nothing tells the leaves what to do" is gone, because the simulation now tells the real leaf what to do.
"We measured that today, on this table" is gone, because nothing is measured.
The closing line says "designed" where it said "built", because the roof exists as a design, a leaf, and a simulation.
The number is no longer bent against resting alone: a fixed roof shades as well as our leaves at noon, so the honest claim is the same shade in the harsh hours and the winter sun given back.

## 30 seconds, 75 to 90 words

"Picture Rosa, waiting for a bus in Houston in July. Researchers measured seventeen stops there, and found shelters hotter than standing in the open. Why? They trapped the heat, and they never moved, while the sun moves all day. So we designed a roof that moves: three leaves with no hinges, borrowed from a flower. This leaf is real. The stop around it is simulated, from our CAD and one real Houston day. Press play, and watch them both bend into shade."

"Picture Rosa. It is three in the afternoon in July, in Houston, and her bus is twelve minutes away. There is no shade at her stop. There usually is not: only about one in six bus stops in her city has a shelter at all.

So build more shelters? In the summer of twenty twenty three, researchers carried instruments to seventeen Houston bus stops. On open ground the heat stress reading averaged ninety two degrees. The best shelter took almost six degrees off that. And the worst shelter was five degrees hotter than standing in the open. Rosa would have been better off in the sun.

Why would a roof make it worse? That shelter was closed in, acrylic and metal, and the researchers said it trapped the heat like an oven. And every roof has a second problem: the sun moves all day, and the roof never does.

So we went looking for a roof that moves, and we found one in a flower. The bird of paradise bends open under the weight of a bird: no hinge, no joint, nothing to rust or jam. This leaf is real, and it does the same. Bend the spine, and the blade flips sideways.

I will be straight with you. We could not build the whole stop this weekend. So we built the leaf, and we simulated everything around it: the stop from our own CAD, the sun from a public solar dataset, one real Houston July day, run in a minute. The simulation drives this leaf.

Would you press play?

The sun is climbing along its real path. There is a light sensor on that roof, and when the light turns harsh, the leaves bend into shade. Watch the screen, and watch the leaf. Now drag that cloud over the sun. They relax. Take it away. They bend again. It is reacting to the light, not to a clock.

Here is what the simulation says. In the harsh hours of that day, the bent leaves cut the direct sun on Rosa's bench to [X] percent, against [Y] if they never moved. A fixed roof could do that too. What it cannot do is open: across the cool months, our leaves give back [W] percent of the sun that a fixed roof blocks. Those are simulated numbers, from geometry. Nothing here was measured, and that card says what we left out.

But picture the city where this works. A shelter that shades Rosa in July, opens to warm her in January, and lets the air through so it never becomes that oven. One motor. No hinges. The same leaf for a farmworker on her rest break, where the law already asks for full shade.

Rosa still has twelve minutes to wait. We cannot move the sun. So we designed the roof that moves instead."

## Recovery lines

- The real leaf does not move: "The leaf has dropped its link, so watch the screen: it is the same signal." Carry on, say it once.
- The page stalls: "Here is the same run, recorded an hour ago." Switch the app to the recorded fixture.
- Everything dies: play the screen recording, apologise once, keep telling Rosa's story over it.
- "So nothing is built?": "The leaf is built, and all of the software, this weekend. The software would drive a real board unchanged, because the simulator speaks the same serial format. The stop is CAD, and we would rather show an honest simulation than a fake demo."
- A question nobody can answer: "Good question. We did not get to that in twenty four hours, and here is how I would find out."
