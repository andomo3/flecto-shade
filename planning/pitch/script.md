"Picture Elena, a foliage grower near Apopka, Florida.
The University of Florida's table for growers like her lists fifty crops, with shade from thirty percent to ninety.
Even one crop will not sit still: for croton, thirty percent is not enough in summer, and forty seven is too much in winter.
But a shade house gives every bed one number.
Her computer waters one valve at a time, yet one motor pulls up to fifty thousand square feet of roof.
A fine brain, and a blunt hand.
So to change a plant's light, Elena moves the plant.

What if the roof moved instead, bed by bed?
That takes many small parts, and every hinge is a bearing to grease.
So we borrowed a part with no hinge: the Flectofin, a blade that bends open, by ITKE in Stuttgart, and patented.
The fin is theirs, and the logic is standard.
Ours is the size of the decision: a bed, not a building.

Would you press play?
Three beds, three light classes: a fern, a hydrangea, a blueberry.
This is the third of June, twenty twenty three: a real rain gauge, and a satellite's sun for the same place.
Watch the fern.

It has had its light by eleven, and it shuts.
The hydrangea by noon.
The blueberries stay open.
That is the moment it stops being one roof.

Then the afternoon storm, forty two millimetres, from the real gauge.
Under cloth, every bed would get all of it.
Here the hydrangea opens, because its soil is dry.
The blueberry stays shut, because its soil is wet enough.
The fern stays shut, because it opted out.

Same rain, three answers.
One bed used the rain, and one was protected from it.
The University of Florida's own report says water on saturated soil will leach fertilizer.
And opening cost the hydrangea six mol of light it did not want.
That is the trade.
One part did both: it shaded at noon, and let the rain in at three.

The roof did that with nine written rules, in plain Python, on a plain web page with no network.
No AI is in the loop, and the fins never follow the sun.

Here is the whole year.
All of it is simulated: apart from one rain gauge, nothing is measured, and nothing was built.
The irrigation system stays, because eight months of the year are dry.
Glasshouses keep rain out on purpose, so this is a shade house.
The blueberry's shade figure comes from Washington State, not Florida.
We make no claim about yield, or cost, or energy, or wind.
From April to October, the rain alone carried the hydrangeas in six months of seven.
Over the year, about fifty four percent of that zone's water.

No grower has seen this yet.
That is what we want next: one grower near Apopka to tell us where it is wrong.

Elena still has one roof and one sky.
Now every bed gets its own answer."

## If the year is not ready

The demo day and the story come first, and the year is a bonus, see "What comes first" in `../plans/plan-d-louvre-roof.md`.
If the year's checks have not passed, or the year view is not built, these lines are swapped and nothing else changes.
The swap was counted, and the script stays inside its word target.

In the 3 minute script, 31 words out and 35 in, for 503 words:

- Out: "Here is the whole year."
  In: nothing, and the next sentence, "All of it is simulated", is said over the result card.
- Out: "From April to October, the rain alone carried the hydrangeas in six months of seven."
  In: "On this one day, the hydrangea's soil stored thirty five millimetres of that storm, and the other two stored none."
- Out: "Over the year, about fifty four percent of that zone's water."
  In: "The year is the same rules, run every day, and we are still checking it."

The 60 second and the 30 second scripts say no figure from the year, so they do not change.
In the storyboard, beat 7 closes on the result card, and its first sentence becomes the second swap above.
The thirty five millimetres is H3's expected value for the demo day, 34.9, and it is provisional in the same way as every other figure.
"The irrigation system stays, because eight months of the year are dry" is said either way, because it is about the climate and not about our run.

## Recovery lines

- Play does nothing, or the page freezes: "Let me reset the day."
  Press `R`, ask again, say it once.
- The page will not load: "Here is a recorded run of the same day, from the same simulation."
  Switch to the recorded run, and carry on from the beat the screen shows, with the same words.
- The laptop dies: play the screen recording on the phone, apologise once, and keep telling Elena's day over it.
- A horticulture expert opens with an objection: concede before anything else.
  "You are right, and growers keep rain off glasshouse crops on purpose.
  That is why this is a shade house, and why each crop opts in."
- A question nobody can answer: "Good question.
  We did not get to that in twenty four hours, and here is how I would find out."
