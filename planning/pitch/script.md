# Pitch script - [project name]

One sentence: a rider waiting in the Houston sun gets a bus stop roof of hingeless leaves that bend into shade on their own, and we measured the light on the bench beneath it.

Booth or stage: booth, expo style, judges come to the table for a few minutes.
Speaker: abba drives. Clicks: the judge presses play, abba only if they decline. Q&A by area: the engineers take the leaf, the mechanism, and the electronics, abba takes the data, the app, and the numbers.

Placeholders to fill or verify before this is said out loud.
Rosa is an illustrative name and the team may change it.
[X] and [Y] are measured at the event.
The study figures and the one in six come from `story.md`, which lists each source, and each is read once more at the source before Saturday.
If replay mode is in use, the sentence "the sensor moves the leaves" is replaced by "right now the app is driving the leaves from the data".

## 30 seconds, 75 to 90 words

"Picture Rosa, waiting for a bus in Houston in July. Researchers measured seventeen stops there, and found shelters that were hotter than standing in the open. Why? It trapped the heat, and it never moved, while the sun moves all day. So we built a roof that moves: three leaves with no hinges, borrowed from a flower, that feel the sun and bend into shade on their own. Press play, and watch one real Houston day. With the leaves bent, the bench gets [X] percent of the light, against [Y]."

## 60 seconds, 150 to 180 words

"Picture Rosa. It is three in the afternoon in July, in Houston, and her bus is twelve minutes away. Only about one in six stops in her city has a shelter. And when researchers carried instruments to seventeen of those stops, they found this: the worst shelter was five degrees hotter than standing in the open.

Why would a roof make it worse? It was closed in, and it trapped the heat. And the sun moves all day, while a roof never does.

So we went looking for a roof that moves, and we found it in a flower. The bird of paradise bends open with no hinge and no joint. Our leaves do the same: one motor, three leaves, nothing to wear out.

Press play. This is one real Houston summer day, run in a minute. The data moves the sun. The leaves move themselves: cover that sensor with your hand, and watch.

With the leaves bent, [X] percent of the light reaches the bench, against [Y] percent without. We cannot move the sun. So we built the roof that moves."

"Picture Rosa. It is three in the afternoon in July, in Houston, and her bus is twelve minutes away. There is no shade at her stop. There usually is not: only about one in six bus stops in her city has a shelter at all.

So build more shelters? In the summer of twenty twenty three, researchers carried instruments to seventeen Houston bus stops. On open ground the heat stress reading averaged ninety two degrees. The best shelter took almost six degrees off that. And the worst shelter was five degrees hotter than standing in the open. Rosa would have been better off in the sun.

Why would a roof make it worse? That shelter was closed in, acrylic and metal, and the researchers said it trapped the heat like an oven. And every roof has a second problem: the sun moves all day, and the roof never does. A fixed roof is built for one hour. Rosa waits at all the others.

So we went looking for a roof that moves, and we found one in a flower. The bird of paradise bends open under the weight of a bird: no hinge, no joint, nothing to rust or jam. This leaf does the same. Bend the spine, and the blade flips sideways. Three leaves, one small motor, and no moving joints at all.

Now, would you press play?

This is one real summer day in Houston, taken from a public solar dataset, run in one minute. The data moves the sun: that lamp is brightening exactly as the sky did that morning. But nothing tells the leaves what to do. There is a light sensor on the roof, and when the sun gets harsh, the leaves bend into shade on their own. Watch them go.

Do not take my word for it. Put your hand over that sensor. The leaves relax. Take it away. They bend again. It is not a script. It is a roof that feels the sun.

And here is what Rosa gets. There is a second sensor down on the bench, where she would be sitting. With the leaves bent, [X] percent of the light reaches her. With them resting, [Y] percent. We measured that today, on this table. We did not measure temperature, and we did not test wind or rain: that card says so.

But picture the city where this works. A shelter that shades Rosa in July, and opens to let the sun warm her in January. It opens to let the air through, so it never becomes that oven. One motor. No hinges. And the same leaf follows the sun for anyone who cannot leave it: a farmworker on her rest break, where the law already asks for full shade, or a grower's crop at midday.

Rosa still has twelve minutes to wait. We cannot move the sun. So we built the roof that moves instead."

## Recovery lines

- The leaves do not respond: "Rooms like this are bright, so let me trigger it by hand." Press the override, carry on, say it once.
- Replay mode is on: "Right now the app is driving the leaves from the data, because this room fools the sensor. Here is a recording of the sensor doing it this morning."
- The board or the cable dies: "Here is this morning's run, recorded from the same sensors." Switch the app to the recorded fixture.
- Everything dies: play the backup video, apologise once, keep telling Rosa's story over it.
- A question nobody can answer: "Good question. We did not get to that in twenty four hours, and here is how I would find out."
