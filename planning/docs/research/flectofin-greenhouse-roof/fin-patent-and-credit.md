# Whose fin it is, and how the team credits it

## Answer

The fin is not the team's.
It is the Flectofin, by ITKE at the University of Stuttgart, and its patent is held by the universities of Stuttgart and Freiburg.
The team's work is the roof logic, zone by zone, and the simulation.
Every page, every card, and every script says so first and unasked.

## The patent

- EP2320015, "Hingeless, infinitely deformable folding mechanism".
- Held by Universitaet Stuttgart and Albert Ludwigs Universitaet Freiburg.
- Inventors: Schleicher, Lienhard, Knippers, Poppinga, Masselter, and Speck.
- Granted 2020-09-23.
- Its stated fields are "shading and privacy systems in architecture, ventilation flaps, opening or closing flaps, as well as adaptive folding mechanisms for aerospace engineering, engines and vehicle technology, medicine, process engineering, and interior decoration or furniture".
- Google Patents shows it as lapsed in most European countries for unpaid renewal fees.
  That is a database entry and not legal advice, and nobody on the team says anything about freedom to use it.

## Where it has been built

- The One Ocean pavilion, Yeosu 2012: 108 kinetic lamellas in glass fibre reinforced polymer, between 3 and 13 m high, architect soma, facade engineering by Knippers Helbig, biomimetic research by ITKE Stuttgart.
- That is a building facade.
  We found no prior proposal to put a Flectofin over crops, and those are the words the team uses.

## How it is credited

- On the page, in the footer: "The fin is the Flectofin, by ITKE, University of Stuttgart, patented as EP2320015."
- In speech: "The fin is not ours. It is ITKE's Flectofin, and it is patented."
- In the submission and on the card "Not ours": both universities, and the paper below.
- Nothing is claimed about how the fin itself behaves, because nothing physical was built.
  The simulation treats a zone's roof as one open fraction.
