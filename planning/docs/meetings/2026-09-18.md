# Meeting 2026-09-18

The engineers wrote an hour by hour build plan for what they can finish at the venue, and the team lead adopted it as the MVP.
It overrides `project-brief.md`, `RUN.md`, and `AGENTS.md` wherever they disagree, because the people building the hardware decide what the hardware is.

## Decisions

- The engineers' 17 hour plan is the MVP, because it is what the people building it have decided they can build, and a plan the builders do not own does not get built.
- One bus stop is the plan.
  The fixed control roof stays as a backup, an hour of foam board built only if time allows, because it turns the light number into adaptive against fixed.
- The ideal sun is the LEDs on a servo mount, so the sun moves across the stop.
  It depends on build time and on the parts being available at the venue, so the pivot is an LED in a fixed mount, and the rig, the firmware, and the app must all work either way.
  The five marked positions are dropped.
- The control law is a threshold on one photoresistor: bright light, the leaves flare.
  The flared state is the shading state, because a roof that opens when the sun comes out is backwards for a bus stop, to be confirmed with the engineers.
- The leaf is a backbone with a lamina that buckles, starting at a 2 mm backbone and a 0.5 mm lamina with a slight built in resting curve as the imperfection seed.
  Two or three thickness combinations are printed or cut and hand tested, and the best one is picked at hour two.
- Three leaves share one backbone and one servo through a pull wire or rigid link.
  Hour six is the last point to simplify to two leaves.
- A flex sensor on the backbone is logged against the commanded servo position.
  It is a rough indicator and never a control input, because the plan says not to over invest in it.
- Light is the primary measure: the light at the bench under the canopy as a percent of the incident light, read live from a second light sensor on the bench.
  Temperature is secondary, a DS18B20 or a thermal camera under the canopy against an open control spot, shown if it reads and never relied on.
- A manual override or backup trigger is prepared and tested, because the judging room's ambient light may make the automatic response unreliable.
- If a mechanical step runs long, time is borrowed from the software layer first.
  A hand triggered canopy that buckles reliably beats an automated one that does not.
- The software's part: the app sets the LED brightness, and the sun's angle when the servo mount exists, to simulate the light at a chosen time or place, and only the photoresistor drives the leaves.
- The demo fast forwards one real day from the hourly dataset, the sunniest Houston day in the file, in about a minute.
  The data moves the sun and only the photoresistor moves the leaves, because a judge can then cover the sensor and see it is not a script.
  Driving the leaves from the data is the pivot, called replay mode, if the threshold is unreliable in the room.
- The dataset is the PVGIS hourly file with pvlib for the sun's position, see [the dataset choice](../research/adaptive-bus-stop-canopy/dataset-choice.md).
- Every pivot has an agreed hour, in the table below.
- Abba builds the software and no hardware.
- Names per role and the project name are not decided yet.
- The team is three people, because one member backed out.
  The work still runs in parallel, so the seven roles are re-split across three at the next meeting.

## The engineers' plan, by venue hour

| Hours | Step | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Design two or three backbone and lamina thickness combinations, queue the prints first, prep the fixed mount jig, hand flex each variant as it comes off | Hour 2: best thickness combination picked, or the top one or two reprinted with an adjusted ratio |
| 2 to 4 | One servo on a breadboard with sweep code, the validated piece attached by pull wire or rigid link, travel and speed tuned so it reaches full flare without stalling or overshooting | Hour 4: one leaf, fully servo actuated, repeatable across several cycles |
| 4 to 6 | The full three leaf, one backbone, one servo assembly | Hour 6: three leaves working reliably, or simplify to two |
| 6 to 8, in parallel | The foam board rig: the front view frame of the bus stop, the LED sun mount, mount points for the leaf assembly and the servo box | The LED has a clear line of sight to where the photoresistor will sit |
| 8 to 9 | Flex sensor on the backbone, readings logged against commanded servo positions | It tracks the bend angle roughly, or it stays a rough indicator |
| 9 to 12 | Photoresistor wired, threshold logic, tested against the LED sun, the sun position and weather stretch only if time allows | Hard checkpoint, hour 12: LED on, leaves respond, end to end |
| 12 to 14 | Everything mounted into the foam board rig, temperature sensors under the canopy and at an open control spot, wiring secured | First full end to end test in the demo rig |
| 14 to 15 | Debug what broke moving from the bench to the rig | The full cycle verified: LED trigger, leaf response, visible temperature differential |
| 15 to 17 | The demo run three to five times back to back, servo pacing tuned for the pitch, the manual override tested, a final rehearsal with the battery charged and the wiring secured for transport | The demo is consistent |

## Pivots

| Failure | Decided at | Pivot |
|---|---|---|
| No leaf variant buckles cleanly | hour 2 | Adjust the thickness ratio and reprint the top one or two, and at hour 4 fall back to a foam board or flat cut lamina on the best backbone |
| The servo cannot reproduce the hand buckle | hour 4 | Change the pull wire or link geometry, and at hour 5 the leaf is hand actuated for the demo |
| Three leaves bind or fall out of sync | hour 6 | Two leaves, then one |
| The servo mounted sun has no parts or no time | hour 8 | The fixed LED mount, the app keeps the brightness control and hides the angle control |
| The flex sensor is noisy | hour 9 | Indicator only, or dropped, the contract field stays empty |
| The light threshold is unreliable in room light | hour 12 | A shroud on the photoresistor, then replay mode: the app commands the leaves from the day's data, and the pitch says so out loud |
| The bench light sensor shows no clear difference | hour 13 | Move or shroud the sensor, then the number becomes "N of N cycles" with the flex trace as proof |
| Temperature shows nothing | hour 14 | The tile is hidden, nothing else changes |
| The backup fixed roof | hour 12 | Built only if the core loop is green and someone is free, otherwise never |
| The servo browns out the board | hour 2, first servo test | The servo moves to its own 5 V supply with a common ground, the engineers believe this is already covered |
| The UNO Q does not run the sketch | hour 1 | The Uno R3, the Arduino challenge dropped |
| The serial link is dead at the table | any time | The app replays the recorded fixture and the hardware runs standalone |
| The rig breaks in transport | 07:00 Sunday | The backup video, recorded after the first clean run |
